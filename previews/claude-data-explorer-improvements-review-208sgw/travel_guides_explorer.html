ABOUT THIS EXPLORER

An exploration tool for data from six African American travel guides, extracted from page scans, 1930 – 1962.

This tool lets you browse structured data extracted from seven travel guides published for Black Americans between 1930 and 1962 — guides that helped Black travelers find welcoming hotels, restaurants, and businesses during the era of racial segregation in the United States.

The publications

These seven guides are the lesser-known counterparts to The Negro Motorist Green Book, published annually by Victor H. Green & Co. from 1936 to 1966 and browsable in its own explorer. Read the full history on Wikipedia →

This explorer searches across seven publications at once. Colors mark each publication throughout the site. Each title links to the source scans in NYPL Digital Collections:

  • Hackley & Harrison’s Hotel and Apartment Guide 1930
  • The Travelers Guide 1931
  • Smith’s Tourist Guide 1940
  • Travelguide 1947–1962
  • Go, Guide to Pleasant Motoring 1952–1959
  • Afro-American’s Travel Guide 1954–1958
  • NHA Directory and Guide to Travelers 1959

Page scans are held by the New York Public Library Digital Collections. Entries were extracted from those scans using an OCR and named-entity-recognition pipeline. The Explorer uses the IIIF standard extensively — entry thumbnails are cropped from the original page images, and each entry’s source link opens the IIIF viewer at the exact page location.

A note on accuracy

This dataset carries OCR and entity-extraction errors, so always treat the original published pages as the authoritative source. This explorer is a way to engage with the directories more deeply, not a replacement for them. Data has not been reviewed for 100% accuracy.

How to use this site

  1. Filter by edition, state, city, or category in the sidebar — counts update live.
  2. Use the search bar to find names, addresses, or notes across all editions at once.
  3. Click any row to open the full entry alongside a thumbnail of the source scan.
  4. Use Export CSV to download the currently filtered selection for your own work.
