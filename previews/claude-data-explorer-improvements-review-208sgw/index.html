A directory of places that welcomed Black travelers, 1930 – 1966.

The Negro Motorist Green Book wasn't the only travel guide for African Americans listing hotels, restaurants, beauty parlors, and other businesses safe to visit during the Jim Crow era of segregation. Every digitized volume of eight such publications has been transcribed into structured records, and every listing links back to the exact spot on the scanned page it was printed on.

About the data

Machine-extracted from the original scans

The page images were transcribed with OCR, and each individual business listing was extracted from that text with a large language model, by the directory-pipeline project. Every entry records the business name, address, city, state and category as printed, along with a IIIF canvas reference locating it on the page — which is what lets any listing be opened at the exact region of the scan it came from.

A note on accuracy

This data carries occasional OCR, entity-extraction, and entry-alignment errors. Always treat the original published pages as the authoritative source — the explorers are a way to engage with the directories more deeply, not a replacement for them. The corpus also reaches only as far as digitization has: other Black travel guides that have not been digitized are not represented here, so the counts describe the digitized record, not the full historical one.

Reuse

The data is public domain

Download

The combined dataset is published as CC0 on Hugging Face: green-books-travel-guides, with a cropped snippet image for every entry in green-books-thumbnails. The raw CSVs are also served directly from this site.

Cite

Citation metadata is in CITATION.cff; GitHub's Cite this repository button exports it to BibTeX or APA. No permission is needed to reuse the data, but a citation helps others find the source.

Build on it

The site and the extraction pipeline are both open: green-books and directory-pipeline.
